# FRC Team 5669 Member Handbook

Version October 20, 2026

## At a glance

You earn your place on Team 5669 every month. To stay on the team, you meet three standards. The FRC app shows how you are doing on each one at any time.

{{diagram:standards}}

- **80% of team meetings each calendar month.** There are two required meetings a week.
- **2 hours of signed-in work every week,** and **10 hours every week of build season.**
- **2.0 GPA** at every progress report and every quarter.

Miss a standard and you go on probation, a fixed warning period. It lasts 4 weeks for meetings or hours, and until the next grade check for grades. Miss meetings or hours again during that probation, or miss the training deadline, and you are removed. You may apply again next season.

## 1. Start here

**What FRC is.** FRC is the FIRST Robotics Competition. Each January, FIRST gives high school teams a new game. Each team builds a large robot to play it, then competes at events.

**Who we are.** Team 5669, the Techmen, is Bosco Tech's FRC team. Our students design, build, wire and program the whole robot, bumpers included. FRC is a varsity activity at Bosco Tech, and we run it like one against California's best teams.

- **Who runs the team:** Mr. Pina runs the team, and everything goes through him. Mr. Garza helps in the background. Seraj Arteaga and Marcos Posada are the student team leaders.
- **No robotics experience is needed** to apply.
- **This handbook is the rulebook.** If someone tells you something different, follow the handbook.

## 3. Why the team exists

Team 5669 turns Bosco Tech students into engineers who can design, build, lead and speak for the school. The robot is how we test it.

- **Membership is a privilege and a commitment,** like a varsity sport. Expect work outside class every week.
- **Students build the robot.** Mentors teach, check and keep you safe. The work and the credit are yours.
- **In team gear, you represent** the team, Bosco Tech, our sponsors and yourself, at events and online.

- **Hours:** mentor and shop time are limited, so they go to members who use them.
- **Grades:** a student failing a class misses the season anyway. A progress report catches it early.
- **Certifications:** no project is worth a finger or an eye.

## 6. Attendance and absences

Being in the room is not enough. You are present when you are on a task and working.

- **Check in** by tapping your phone on any of the three team NFC tags: next to the IDEA Classroom entrance, in the IDEA side entrance hallway, or left of the shop's double doors, in the first 15 minutes of a meeting.
- **After that you are late. No scan is an absence.** Three lates in a calendar month count as one absence, and lates reset on the 1st.
- **No phone, or the tag fails?** Tell a mentor right away. The mentor checks you in or out in the app and logs why.
- **Excused absences:** ask in the app at least 24 hours ahead. A mentor approves or denies it. An approved absence never counts against you.
- **A request still unanswered at the meeting** is approved.
- **Can be excused:** illness, a family emergency, a required school event, a medical visit, a religious holiday, or a test, a major assignment or a college application deadline asked for ahead.
- **Plan other activities around team meetings.**
- **An emergency** is your own illness or injury, or a death, hospital stay or serious illness in your close family. File it by 11:59 PM the next school day. Sick? Stay home.
- **We never round.** With 8 or 9 meetings in a month, you can miss one. 7 of 9 is under 80%.
- **A week you are fully excused** never counts against your hours.

## 7. Hours and sessions

- **2 hours of signed-in work every week, 10 in build season.** Any time the shop is open counts, official or not.
- **Volunteer hours: 10 a year.** FLL mentoring and community events count, apart from weekly hours.
- **Sponsors:** every member helps find them. The business and outreach sub-team leads.

### Signing in and out

{{diagram:session}}

1. **Scan the tag when you arrive.** Your session is all your time in the building, breaks and food included. Here before the meeting? Scan again in its first 15 minutes.
2. **Pick a task** from your sub-team's list in the app.
3. **To leave, scan and leave a note** of what you did, typed or spoken. The note asks to leave. Spoken notes become text, and the audio is not kept.
4. **Wait for approval** from a mentor, or a student lead Mr. Pina permits. Your hours stop when it is approved. If your area still needs cleaning, you clean first. Any adult who opened the shop can approve. A lead never approves their own.

- **Leave without approval,** or forget to scan out, and you keep the meeting credit but lose that session's hours.
- **A session open 12 hours** is flagged for a mentor to fix or void.
- **Idle time is voided.** No task, or not working on it? A lead or mentor voids the session and sends you the reason.
- **Everyone signs in, every time,** leaders, drive team and mentors too.
- **Scan only for yourself.** Faking hours is a conduct violation, up to removal. The first time voids both students' hours that week and is a written warning.
- **FLL:** scan and choose Switch to FLL. The build session ends there and counts. Sign out of FLL with a note; no approval needed.
- **Clean 10:** you clean up your own mess. See a dirty area? Clean it, whoever left it. We share the shop, so we clean it together. Skipping cleanup is what counts against you: a mentor can void that day's hours. Everyone stays to the end and cleans together for 10 minutes when a mentor calls it. The time changes day to day. Leaving before then needs a mentor's OK, and you clean your area first.

### Make your time count

- Check your task list before you arrive. Stuck 10 minutes? Ask your lead. Blocked? Take the next task.
- Before you leave, finish the task or note where you stopped, so the next person can pick it up.
- Only one session this week? Take a task you can finish in it.
- **No task? Train.** SolidWorks has free training, and our sponsorship opens its certification exams. Finding work is your job.

### Take care of yourself

- **Keep time for life outside robotics.**
- **Look out for teammates.** If someone seems overwhelmed, tell a mentor.
- **Struggling?** Talk to a mentor or a Bosco Tech counselor. Asking never counts against you. In a crisis, call or text 988.

## 8. Shop safety

Safety comes before every deadline, match and award. The shop is a privilege, not a right.

- **The shop opens only with an adult on site,** and an adult is there whenever a machine runs. Mr. Pina, Mr. Garza, Mr. Cosso or any Bosco Tech staff member with keys and permission opens it.
- **Other adults may help supervise** while one of those adults is present (Section 20).
- **No machine tools on the day the schedule marks** (Section 4).
- **Safety glasses** when you use a tool, or anyone within 5 feet of you does. The team lends glasses to anyone who needs them, but owning your own safety gear is strongly recommended, for hygiene and convenience. Laser and welding stations need the eye protection posted there.
- **Dress for the shop:** closed-toe shoes, long hair tied back, no loose clothes, hoodie strings or dangling jewelry. No fleece or other synthetics when cutting or grinding steel or titanium; sparks melt them.
- **Stop when unsure, tired, or on medicine that dulls your judgment.** A safety stop is never a failure.
- **Report at once** every injury, near miss, damaged tool or battery problem to a mentor.
- **No horseplay.** **Tools stay in the shop** unless a mentor approves.
- **Return every tool and clean your area.** Every sign-out asks you to confirm it. Leave it dirty and a mentor can void that day's hours or suspend your certification for that station.
- **No food or drink,** except water. **Team computers are for team work:** no new software or settings without your lead's OK.
- **Visitors** need a mentor's OK and stay with the member who invited them. Parents are always welcome.
- **Any mentor can stop work or close the shop** for safety, at any time.
- **Safety violations:** a mentor logs each one and your family is told. Each counts against leadership, drive team and letters.
- **The third in a school year goes to Mr. Pina,** who decides if you lose tools, competition roles or your place. He decides within 5 school days; no tools until then.

## 9. Medical and emergencies

**If someone is hurt**

1. **Stop and make the area safe.** Anyone may hit an emergency stop.
2. **Get the nearest adult.** For a serious injury, the adult calls 911 first, then gives first aid.
3. **Log every injury and near miss** in the app the same day.
4. **The adult calls the parent** and tells the Bosco Tech front office the same day.

- **Emergency information:** a parent keeps two emergency contacts, medical conditions, allergies and medicines current in the app. Only staff mentors see it.
- **At every event,** a staff mentor carries every member's emergency contacts and medical notes.
- **Carry your own inhaler or epinephrine,** and tell your lead where it is. Bosco Tech's medicine forms apply: get them from the front office.
- **SAFE-1 shows** where the first aid kit, eyewash, fire extinguisher and nearest AED (heart defibrillator) are. One AED is in the front office.
- **Meal sign-ups list the team's allergies,** with no names.
- **A mentor who learns a student may be at risk** tells a counselor and the parents that day.

## 10. Training and certifications

A certification proves you can run a machine or do a skill alone, with zero help. Run a machine alone only if you hold its IDEA certification. The rules for each are at [ideabosco.com/certifications](https://ideabosco.com/certifications).

- **Four steps:** training, a guided project, a test while a teacher watches, and a solo project.
- **SAFE-1 and RESP-1 come first** and are renewed every year. Renew them by the first meeting of the school year.
- **Sub-team training** is your sub-team's list of certifications in the app. It is due before Kickoff. Miss it and you are removed.
- **This season only SAFE-1 and RESP-1 are required.** Earn every other one you can before Kickoff. More become required later.
- **A sign-off means a trainer watched you do it.** A video or helping someone else does not count.
- **Veterans train rookies,** and the off-season is for training on real projects, like practice robots.

## 11. Standing, probation and removal

Your standing is your status: good standing, probation or removed. The FRC app shows yours.

{{diagram:standing}}

| Standing | Why | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |

- **It closes after 4 clean weeks.** A fully excused week does not count, so probation runs a week longer.
- **Grades probation** keeps the normal attendance rules and removes drive team and pit crew until the next grade check. Still under 2.0 then? A new one starts.
- **Two probations at once** run and close separately.

- **Every month, Mr. Pina confirms** each probation and removal.

## 14. Conduct, discipline and reporting

Three sets of rules apply at every team activity, on campus and off. They are the Bosco Tech student handbook, the FIRST Code of Conduct and this handbook. Bosco Tech's rules come first.

**The [FIRST Code of Conduct](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program), in short:** show Gracious Professionalism: compete hard, and stay kind and respectful. Keep everyone safe, and report anything unsafe. FIRST can bar anyone who breaks it.

### What we expect

- **Respect everyone:** teammates, mentors, other teams, volunteers and the public.
- **No bullying, harassment, slurs, demeaning jokes or swearing,** in person or online.
- **Sexual harassment** in any form is a serious violation: comments, jokes, images, messages or touching. Mentors report it to Bosco Tech that day. Never make, keep or forward sexual images of anyone.
- **No hazing, and nobody is treated worse for who they are.** Bosco Tech's non-discrimination policy applies.
- **No alcohol, vaping, drugs or weapons** at any team activity, carpools and hotels included.
- **Own mistakes quickly.** Hiding a problem is worse than the problem. Honest mistakes are never blamed.
- **Phones only for team work** during sessions. **No romantic touching** at team events.
- **Never misrepresent your role,** including on college applications.
- **In doubt?** Ask whether anyone would object if every parent, mentor and teammate read about it.

### Discipline

{{diagram:discipline}}

1. **Verbal warning** from a mentor, naming the rule, logged in the app.
2. **Written warning,** recorded in the app with the rule broken. It stays on your record for the season, and your parents are told.
3. **Suspension** from all team activities for 1 to 4 weeks, end date in the app. Parents are told. A suspended member misses every team activity, the home district event included. Those weeks count neither for nor against the standards.
4. **Removal** from the team.

- **Serious violations skip steps,** up to immediate removal. They include violence, threats, harassment, theft, youth protection breaches, drugs or alcohol, and faking hours twice.
- **Appeal a written warning** to Mr. Pina within 5 school days. It stands until he decides. A parent may join if the family wants.
- **Suspension or removal:** talk it through with Mr. Pina; a parent may join. Mr. Pina makes the final decision.
- **Knowing these rules is your job.**
- **School discipline matters** go to Bosco Tech's Dean of Discipline, as during the school day.

### Reporting

- **Tell any mentor, or any Bosco Tech teacher, counselor or administrator.** At an event, tell a mentor while you are there.
- **You never get in trouble for reporting,** or for asking if something is OK.
- **You never have to tell team mentors first.** You can go straight to Bosco Tech staff or FIRST Youth Protection.
- **Mentors are mandated reporters:** the law requires us to report possible abuse or harm, so we cannot keep it secret.
- **Getting back at someone for reporting means removal.**
- **Tell a trusted adult right away if any adult:**
  - asks you to keep a secret
  - asks you to talk on another app, or with no other adults around
  - gives you gifts or offers you alcohol or drugs
  - wants to meet outside robotics

### Conflicts between members

1. **Talk to the person directly,** calmly and in private.
2. **Not settled in a week? Go to a mentor,** who meets with you both.
3. **Then a Bosco Tech counselor or administrator.**

If talking feels unsafe, or it involves harassment or safety, go straight to a mentor or Bosco Tech staff.

## 16. Competitions and travel

Competition is an off-site team workday with the whole FRC community watching. Every member attends every competition. Drive team and pit crew spots are earned, never guaranteed. Most events are close to home. The rules below cover the rest, like Beach Blitz or a championship.

{{diagram:roster}}

### Who goes

- **Every event needs a family sign-up** in the app. No sign-up, no event. For a mandatory event, a mentor reminds the family first. Still missing 48 hours later? It is an unexcused absence.
- **FIRST registration and the Consent and Release** are done before any event.
- **Mentors pick the pit crew and drive team** for each event. They weigh reliability, hours, attendance, conduct, safety record and team needs, and record why.
- **Jobs fill first:** drive team, pit crew, Impact presenters, pit speakers, then scouts.
- **Not eligible** for the drive team, pit crew or speaking for the team: members on probation, and anyone who missed Kickoff without an emergency. They still attend every competition. Suspended members do not attend. Eligibility is checked when the event starts.
- **Declare conflicts** by February 1 for spring events, and at sign-up for fall events.
- **The roster is posted at least 10 days before** each event.

### Getting there

- **Parents drive in carpools,** with no school transportation. Not every family has to drive. Cars leave the Bosco Tech front lot as each fills, and the carpool board shows every car.
- **The carpool plan locks 72 hours before leaving.** After that only a mentor can change it, and nothing changes in the last 12 hours.
- **Riding with another family** needs both families' OK in the app, with mentors copied. No car carries one adult alone with one student not their own.
- **Drivers** are licensed adults named in the family sign-up. Before a first ride, a driver uploads one photo of their license and insurance to the FRC app. It is stored privately, seen only by staff mentors, and deleted when the driver asks or leaves the team. Driving earns parent service hours.
- **Students do not drive other students** to team events.
- **Required costs:** the annual uniform from FRC Tees, a team shirt and jacket at FRC Tees' price, ordered through the team (details in the app), and, for manufacturing work, a respirator: a 3M 7500 series half facepiece in your size with 3M 60926 cartridges, about $45 to $65. It is the same one IDEA classes require, so buy it once. Families pay their own lodging and food. Cost a problem? A parent emails Mr. Pina, and it stays private.
- **Meals:** events are potluck; each family brings food to share. Weekend sessions get food from a parent sign-up, or the team orders pizza.
- **Travel on a meeting day?** The meeting becomes the trip briefing.

### At the event

- **Arrive before the doors open** each morning, by the time in the app. The line decides where we sit.
- **Stay for the whole event,** and leave the venue only with a mentor's OK.
- **Your role comes first:** pit, scouting, drive team. Socializing and shirt trading come after. Everyone without another role scouts.
- **Wear this year's team shirt,** and any jacket must be team gear. Never cut, change or sell team clothes; trading with other FRC teams is fine.
- **Around the drive team,** no talk of rankings, stats or results. Keep it calm.
- **Help other teams** and leave the pit cleaner than you found it.

### Overnight events

Families book their own hotel rooms, so at the hotel your family is in charge of you, not the team. Team adults never room with or supervise one student alone. When the team reserves rooms and names chaperones, these rules apply.

- **Curfew is 10:00 PM,** in your room.
- **Leaving the hotel:** groups of 3 or more, with a chaperone's OK. Ask in the travel chat: who, where, and when you will be back. No answer means no.
- **No visiting another student's room.** Meet in a space a chaperone approves, door wide open.
- **Students share rooms** only with students of the same gender, or with family.
- **Two on-call adults, a man and a woman,** are named in the trip plan.
- **Students never go into an adult's hotel room.**
- **A serious violation means going home,** at the family's expense.

## 20. Adults on the team: youth protection

Regular mentors are screened before they work with students. Occasional parent helpers and carpool-only drivers are not, and they help only at activities where a screened mentor is present. Every adult follows this section and Sections 8, 14 and 17.

- **Screening:** FIRST Youth Protection screening, a California Live Scan fingerprint check, and mandated reporter training. To start, contact Mr. Pina.
- **A regular mentor** helps more than 16 hours a month or 32 a year. Anyone who supervises with no screened mentor present is one too.
- **Occasional helpers need nothing extra.** A parent who comes in for a day or two helps while a screened mentor is in the room.
- **Carpool drivers** need Live Scan only past 16 hours a month or 32 a year, as California law requires.
- **Alumni may mentor right after graduating,** once screened like any regular mentor. They are never occasional helpers. Alumni under 21 never supervise students alone and stay out of student-only chats.
- **Rule of Three:** no adult is ever alone with one student, in a room, a car or a message. Every conversation includes at least three people. The only exception is a parent with their own child.
- **Messages:** an adult never messages one student privately. A direct message includes a second adult, or moves to a group channel.
- **The last two students leave together,** so no student is the last one alone with an adult. A student whose ride is late waits with a buddy.
- **At least one adult for every 25 students** at team events.
- **No dating or romantic contact between an adult and a student, ever,** alumni included.
- **Physical contact** is limited to high fives, fist bumps, handshakes and first aid. No hugs.
- **Parent mentors step out of decisions about their own child:** acceptance, roles, travel and discipline.
- **Mentors teach, then step back,** from showing, to helping, to watching. They never belittle a student.

## 21. Joining and reapplying

Applications are open all year. Every returning member reapplies each May. A spot this year does not carry into next year.

- **Returning members** are judged on this season's attendance, hours, contribution and conduct.
- **FRC work in IDEA class is not membership.** Class participants are not on the roster, do not travel and do not earn letters. To join, apply and meet every standard here.
- **Every member gets an individual interview** with Mr. Pina each season. A parent may join if the family wants.
- **Joining after Kickoff?** Finish the checklist and sub-team training within 3 weeks. You are not counted as missing Kickoff.
- **Removed students** may reapply in the next spring cycle. Acceptance can include a meeting with Mr. Pina and a parent.

## 22. How this handbook works

- **Every member, parent and mentor must follow this handbook.**
- **If two rules cover the same thing, the stricter one applies.** Nothing here overrides a Bosco Tech rule.
- **Mentors may update it during the season,** and every change is announced in the app. A change takes effect 7 days later; a safety change, at once.
- **Major changes are re-signed in the app:** eligibility, travel, safety, costs or conduct.
- **Every version is numbered,** and the app records who signed which version, and when.
- **Reviewed every spring** by mentors and team leaders, after every member says in the app what worked and what to change.
- **Mentors may go past the written rules only to protect safety or wellbeing.** Deadlines and the three standards are never judgment calls.
- **A conflict that existed before this handbook** (October 20, 2026) gets a written plan with Mr. Pina through the end of that activity's season.

## 23. Glossary

- **Armory:** the team's online storage for CAD files.
- **Build season:** the weeks from Kickoff in January to our last competition, when the team meets more often.
- **Bumpers:** padded frames that protect the robot and show our number.
- **CAD:** computer-aided design, done in SolidWorks before we build.
- **Chaperone:** an adult the team names to be in charge on a trip.
- **Chief Delphi:** a public website where FRC teams talk about robots.
- **District event:** an FRC competition in our area.
- **Drive team:** the students who run the robot in matches, picked by the drive coach.
- **FACTS:** Bosco Tech's grade system, where official grades are kept.
- **FIRST:** the nonprofit that runs FRC, FLL and other robotics programs.
- **FLL:** FIRST LEGO League, robotics for younger students.
- **FRC:** the FIRST Robotics Competition, the high school league we compete in.
- **GitHub:** the website that stores the robot's code.
- **GPA:** grade point average, from your grades in FACTS.
- **Gracious Professionalism:** FIRST's way to compete: play hard, and stay kind and respectful.
- **IDEA:** Bosco Tech's engineering, design and manufacturing program.
- **Impact Award:** FRC's top award, for a team's effect on its community.
- **Kickoff:** the January day FIRST reveals the new game.
- **Live Scan:** California's fingerprint background check for adults who work with students.
- **Mandated reporter:** an adult the law requires to report possible abuse or harm.
- **Mentor:** an adult who guides the team: Mr. Pina, Mr. Garza, or a screened mentor listed in the app.
- **NFC tag:** a small chip sticker you tap with your phone.
- **Off-season:** the months between the last competition and Kickoff.
- **Pit, pit crew:** our work area at an event, and the students who fix the robot there.
- **Probation:** a fixed warning period after you miss a standard.
- **Progress report:** Bosco Tech's grade report in the middle of each quarter.
- **RESP-1:** the IDEA certification for cleanup and tool care.
- **Roster:** the list of members, or of members going to an event.
- **Rule of Three:** no adult is ever alone with one student; every conversation includes at least three people.
- **SAFE-1:** the IDEA certification for shop safety.
- **Scouting:** watching other teams' matches and recording how they do.
- **Sponsor:** a company or person who gives the team money, parts or services.
- **Station:** one machine or work area in the shop.
- **Sub-team:** the group you belong to, like Programming or Electrical.
- **Sub-team lead:** the student the app names to lead a sub-team.
- **Travel:** going to a competition away from Bosco Tech. Every member goes.
- **Varsity letter:** a Bosco Tech award for a full season at a varsity standard.
- **Voided:** removed. Voided hours do not count.
